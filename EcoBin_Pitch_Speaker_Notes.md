## Slide 2 — The Problem: Waste is an Escalating Operational & Economic Burden
**Headline:** Unsegregated Waste Drives Crushing Logistics Overhead and Environmental Decay  
**Target Duration:** 35 Seconds

### Spoken Pitch:
> "According to the Central Pollution Control Board, India discards over 150,000 metric tons of municipal solid waste every single day. More than half of that is wet organic food waste.
> 
> The core breakdown is not a lack of treatment plants—it is that human manual segregation fails at the source over 40% of the time. The second wet curry or gravy touches paper and plastics, over 80% of recyclable material value is permanently ruined, and conventional digesters choke on stray plastic films.
> 
> Even worse, 50% to 70% of municipal and institutional waste budgets are squandered purely on diesel trucks hauling heavy water weight (food scraps are 70% water) to distant landfills. Under mandatory Solid Waste Management Rules 2016, bulk waste generators now face up to ₹50,000 in NGT penalties if they fail to process wet waste on-site."

**Transition Bridge:** *"So how are campuses and housing societies currently trying to handle this?"*

## Slide 3 — The Current System: How Customers Solve It Today & Municipal Expenses
**Headline:** How Customers Solve Waste Today: Heavy Haulage Expense & Sunk OpEx  
**Target Duration:** 35 Seconds

### Spoken Pitch:
> "Right now, campuses and gated societies rely on a deeply flawed linear pipeline. They put out blue and green colored bins—which fail because voluntary sorting collapses under real-world human behavior. They hire private diesel haulers, spending ₹50,000 to ₹1.5 Lakhs every month just to haul wet scraps off-campus. 
> 
> At the exact same time, their dining kitchens burn through 25 to 40 expensive commercial LPG cylinders a month, costing another ₹50,000 to ₹90,000. They are paying twice: once to buy thermal energy, and once to haul it away!
> 
> When they attempt open composting, pits turn foul and anaerobic during rains, breeding flies and getting rejected when plastics mix in. Conventional biogas units sour within weeks due to lack of automated pH telemetry. The opportunity is obvious: eliminate truck haulage entirely and convert daily food waste into clean cooking fuel right on campus."

**Transition Bridge:** *"Here is what EcoBin does and who we target."*

## Slide 4 — Our Solution: What We Do & Who We Target
**Headline:** Autonomous Decentralized Bio-Utility: What We Do & Who We Target  
**Target Duration:** 35 Seconds

### Spoken Pitch:
> "EcoBin deploys autonomous edge-AI source-segregation coupled with closed-loop anaerobic biodigestion, converting daily food waste directly into free cooking fuel and pristine recyclables right on campus.
> 
> Our immediate beachhead is university campuses and student hostels—tailored directly for institutional dining halls. With 1,000 to 3,000 students generating 150 to 400 kg of daily plate waste, the Campus Director is a single decision-maker focused on eliminating ₹45,000 monthly hauling bills and earning NAAC A++ Green Campus credentials.
> 
> Our scaled market expands to gated residential societies (RWAs), where 200 to 1,200 apartments spend dedicated maintenance funds to avoid ₹50,000 municipal fines and eliminate foul dumpster bays.
> 
> We achieve this through four core innovations: sub-150ms edge-AI vision classification preserving recyclable purity, in-situ stainless-steel micro-slurry maceration (<2mm), active anti-souring bio-telemetry with automated pH buffer dosing, and direct piping of clean methane (65% CH4) into kitchen stoves."

**Transition Bridge:** *"Let's examine how this functions as an integrated closed-loop utility."*

## Slide 5 — Structured Product Model & Business Model
**Headline:** Structured Product & Business Model: Closed-Loop Utility Pipeline  
**Target Duration:** 35 Seconds

### Spoken Pitch:
> "On the left is our closed-loop campus architecture. In student mess halls and residential floors, users simply drop mixed trash into our Smart Segregation Nodes. Optical and dielectric sensors split the waste in under 150 milliseconds: the Blue Pipe routes dry recyclables to an on-site baler, while food scraps are macerated into micro-slurry and routed through the Green Pipe into a central mesophilic dome digester. The purified methane directly fires the dining stoves, while pathogen-safe mineral digestate fertilizes campus landscaping.
> 
> Our business model is equally structured. Campuses and societies invest in modular EcoBin Society hubs via CapEx (₹3 Lakhs to ₹8 Lakhs), backed by a recurring ₹5,000 to ₹10,000 monthly AMC retainer for bio-cultures, pH buffer replenishment, cloud telemetry, and preventive servicing. Because the biogas directly displaces commercial LPG and ends private hauling, campuses achieve full capital payback in just 16 to 20 months."

**Transition Bridge:** *"Let's look at the sheer scale of the addressable market."*

## Slide 7 — Revenue Structure & Unit Economics
**Headline:** Dual-Stream Revenue Model with Compelling Unit Economics  
**Target Duration:** 35 Seconds

### Spoken Pitch:

>

Even after paying our ₹7,500 monthly AMC retainer, the campus pockets ₹1,00,000 in net monthly cash benefit, or ₹12 Lakhs every single year.
